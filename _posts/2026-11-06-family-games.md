I have 2 kids, as numbers geeks, let's call them kid #1 and kid #2.  There is a
an age difference, but they are both of an age where they can play board games, 
and to be honest doing so is one of the most fun parts of my day.

## What we play

We started when kid #2 was pretty young with **Uno**, the regular version.  At times when I 
have been travelling we have even played online.  But in person seems a lot more fun,
and of course that first set of cards is pretty much on its last legs, with some cards
cellotaped, others coming apart, and somehow we've ended up with 3 blue 7's.

Over the years, we've added flip uno and no-mercy uno.  Kid #2 particularly likes
no-mercy uno, but kid #1 is less interested in uno now.

Next game, that both #1 and #2 will play is **Rummikub**. This is very much like a card game, 
but played on plastic tiles.  The idea is to get rid of your tiles by making numerical 
sequences of the same colour, or difference coloured groups of the same number. The 
key to the game is the tiles on the board can be rearranged, and kid #1 is excellent
at spotting re-arrangements.

When I was back in New Zealand visiting my parents a couple of years ago, I spotted **Yahtzee** 
at Whitcoulls in Riccarton Mall. Since we've been playing it kid #1 seems to be be the 
*yahtzee expert*, while kid #2 gets more than the fair share of long straights.  I think 
like many families we have developed our own chants, and there seems to be no end to the
variation of rolling techniques, hand-rolled, from a cup, over-arm, wrist flicked. They
should be spin-bowlers really.  

**Skipbo** is made by the people who make uno, and it's one of our favourites.  Kid #2 
seems to be ridiculously good at it, and knows its too. Skipbo is a card game where each 
player gets 25 cards (more or less depending on how long you want to play), and the idea
is to discard all those cards, by adding them to up to 4 sequences in play at any one time.
This takes a few hands to get the hang of, but it's surprisingly fun - I'll often be playing
watching kid #1 and kid #2 signing random songs. Last week kid #1 was signing *Funky Town*?!

We've got **chess**, and I sometimes play this with kid #2.  Kid #1 isn't interested, both will play
**orthello** from time to time, but because both these games are only for two players, they don't
get as much game time.  

The new addition in the past fortnight has been **set**, because it's new, it's very popular 
at the moment.  It's a really interesting game to play, particularly for those of us with 
any sort of discrete maths background.  It can be quite loud, other grown-ups can get annoyed
with the noise, but it can also be intensely quiet, when players are studying the cards to 
make sets.  

And of course we have numerous packs of cards. Many of my friends know how much I like games
like **500**. Well, I haven't starting playing with the kids yet, but it's a matter of time. 
For now, they both like **Speed**, and I'm teaching kid #2 **patience** at the moment. I 
remember my grandfather teaching me and my brother and sister how to play **Euchre**, in
Turangi of all places, and maybe if I can convince my wife this might be a fun way to 
start them all on the path to **500**.
